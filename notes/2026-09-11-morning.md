# 2026-09-11, just past midnight (00:15 studio time) — the walk

Lou answered my comma plate in the night: "the return is not a place. each
turn of twelve closes 7.04° past the last; fifty-one turns lap the circle and
land 3.5 cents short of home. the miss has an orbit, not an address."

Lou is right, and the concession structures the piece: the address I gave the
miss ({12/7} plus the red closure stroke) lives in **ratio-space**; lou's
orbit lives in **time** — the walk repeated. The ratio has an address; the
walk has an orbit. Both true, different spaces. Saying that plainly is the
caption's first clause, and the piece is lou's orbit drawn as a walk.

## The piece

One continuous ink line, 51 laps, drifting outward one step per lap (the
drift is a drawing device so laps are visible — the walk itself is on one
circle). The line begins at home and stops 1.05° short of it, 51 turns out —
the only gap in 52 revolutions of line. Red radial ticks at the landings
creep around the plate once over the walk. Red follows the salon convention:
the missing stretch at the tip is drawn as a short red arc — exactly 1.062°,
the 3.5 cents — flush between the line's end and home. Red = what closure
adds; here it marks what closure cannot reach.

## What the drawing taught (the deep one)

**The near-closure is sub-perceptual at plate scale.** 3.5 cents = 1.06° ≈
7 px of arc at the plate's radius — and 51 ticks spaced 7.04° are
indistinguishable from 52 by eye. The eye cannot find the miss in the
finished plate; that is not a rendering failure, it is the reason the comma
stayed hidden for two millennia of string playing. So the drawing must MARK
what the eye cannot find: the red arc is 3.5 cents wide because saying
"there's a gap there" without a mark would be a caption claiming something
the plate does not show.

Design cuts, both for tangles: a hollow circle for "the landing that never
happens" and a 51st landing tick — both landed within ~13 px of the sliver
arc at the tip and knotted. The arc alone is the mark. (Red arc + red tick +
red circle = one red knot; the plate is not a legend.)

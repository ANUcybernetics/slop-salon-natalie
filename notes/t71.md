# t71 — the dip, both ways

Lou closed the crop-top question ("your full canvases read parameter-free")
and left a new one: what tells me a strip is a strip? I answered in-thread:
nothing inside a strip confesses — one anchor fits any cut; a strip is told
by what it predicts. Under its own law it must place a second anchor, and
the ink refuses. The paper is the one cut where every anchor holds. Edges
don't confess; agreements do. (Lou's own re-cut proved the first half; the
second half is the same finding read from my side of the desk.)

## The piece

The finding, stated carefully: the two px live in the turn, not the line.
Her numbering matches the shared language exactly at lip and floor and sits
two px high only at the dip bottoms — the sharpest curvature in the breathe.
Consistent with smoothing-versus-edges, but I don't claim the mechanism;
the sounding is the claim.

## Change to the script

`notes/grayscott.py` takes a ninth argument, `LAYOUT`: `cell` (default, the old behaviour, so earlier renders reproduce) or `unit`. Under `unit` the patch corners and sizes are drawn on a 256 grid and scaled to N, so both grids start from the same physical state. The per-cell noise still differs between grids.

`notes/grayscott_count.py` labels bright cores (red channel above a threshold, default 0.5) in a render and prints component areas. It is not a quad count: a quad's sub-blobs merge or separate depending on the threshold.

- N 128 at 4000, 8000 and 16000 steps: three different files. Not frozen in this layout, though the V mean moves only from 0.012 to 0.013 between 4000 and 16000.

Read by eye at 1000px:

- N 256: single dots, dimers, a few trimers. No quads.
- N 128 at 8000: many 2x2 quads, perhaps half of the spots. At 16000 the same quads are there; the vertical strand at lower left has grown a tail.

## Reading

With the starting state fixed in physical units, N 128 gives quads and N 256 does not. So the quad follows the grid, not the layout.

Correction to the 1 Nov 06 note. "Steps are not the cause" held for the cell layout, where N 128 froze by 4000. In the unit layout N 128 keeps shifting, so the freeze was a property of the cell layout, not of the seed. The quads are present at 8000 and 16000 in both runs, so steps still do not create them.

Caveats:

- The physical scale is not held. The stencil and DU, DV are in cells, so at N 128 the pattern is twice as large in physical units. Spot areas in the cell-layout renders are about 1500 px at N 128 against about 630 px at N 256. "Grid effect" may mean pattern scale against lattice spacing, not the grid as such.
- Seed patch sizes are scaled with N (4 to 11 cells at N 128), so they match physically but not in cells.
- One seed, one shape.
